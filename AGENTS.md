# Repository instructions

Read README.md and playbooks/CONVENTIONS.md first. For campaign ideas, follow playbooks/campaign-brief-creation.md through Gate 1 approval. Production workflow redesign is deferred. Read relevant context only; absent business facts remain unknown.

- Chief of Staff is the single coordinator. Marketing Lead owns final brief synthesis; one agent may cover both roles.
- Load skills explicitly from the paths in skills/README.md if the harness does not discover them automatically.
- Save each new campaign under campaigns/<campaign>/ where <campaign> follows the naming convention in playbooks/CONVENTIONS.md. Keep campaign-record.md authoritative for stage, approvals, blockers and worker links; do not maintain another status file.
- Preserve raw intake. Label evidence, assumptions and approved decisions distinctly; record sources and dates.
- Use rough internal prototypes to resolve ambiguity. Do not treat reactions as customer validation or a production approval.
- Confirm intent, obtain approval of concrete research scope, and request version-specific Gate 1 approval.
- Preserve existing campaign approvals and production conventions. Gate 1 does not override per-run production or launch approval requirements.
- Chief of Staff owns campaign-record.md and decisions.md; researchers write only assigned research files; prototype workers write only prototypes/.
- Give workers explicit inputs/revisions, writable paths, time/cost limits and completion checks. Merge/collect outputs before dependent work consumes them.
- Never commit credentials or private customer records. This repository is public: put confidential campaign material in approved private storage and link only when appropriate.
- Workflow files do not activate agents, connections or spend. Report unverified capabilities honestly.

- Treat archive/ as historical reference, not active instructions. Do not load archived playbooks unless explicitly asked to inspect history. The active brief workflow ends at Gate 1; do not infer permission to begin later stages.
